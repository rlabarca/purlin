# Feature: states

> Description: The level and the three evidence levels of a rule, the
>   flags beside them, the rollups over them, the structured payload every
>   surface reads, and the status table a person reads. One reader assembles
>   specs, the evidence and the signatures into the cells of
>   every rule; the table, the dashboard, the gate check and the drift report
>   all render that one payload rather than parsing each other's output.
> Scope: scripts/mcp/purlin/states.py, scripts/mcp/purlin/payload.py, scripts/mcp/purlin/status.py
> Stack: python/stdlib, json, hashlib, subprocess (list-only)

## Rules

- RULE-1: A rule that no proof line names has a passed cell reading `no test` with the one reason `no proof written`, an empty proof list and the `no_proof` flag raised [bar: strong]
- RULE-3: The passed cell reads `passed` when every proof of the rule has a passing test in an evidence section that is current, whose spec, code and tests fingerprint equals the one taken now, and it carries the source, whether the pass is current and whether it counts [bar: strong]
- RULE-4: An evidence file's source is the folder it sits in, `ci` or `local`, and a current section from either counts at every gate, `signed` included, because what a signature locks is the evidence rather than the machine that produced it [bar: strong]
- RULE-5: A current section under either source, committed or not, leaves the passed cell reading `passed` with that source named, `counts` true and no reason at all, at every gate; the runtime proof files a run leaves under `.purlin/runtime/` are not read [bar: strong]
- RULE-42: An evidence file whose own `source` field disagrees with the folder it sits in is left out of the reading, and one warning naming its path is added to the payload's warnings [bar: strong]
- RULE-43: The passed cell carries `platforms`, one entry per operating system a current section covers, each carrying that platform's own word, its source and when it ran; a platform a proof is tagged for that no current section covers is listed with the word `not run` and no source [bar: strong]
- RULE-57: A person's own section answers for its own platform at every gate, so a red local section beside a green `ci` one from another operating system reads `partial` wherever the gate is set [bar: strong]
- RULE-44: Where the platforms disagree the passed cell reads `partial`, naming the platforms that passed and the word of each that did not; `partial` is not met, its bucket is `partial` and its flag is `partial` [bar: strong]
- RULE-45: A section that is out of date decides nothing: where the newest section that speaks for the rule is current, an older out-of-date one beside it is left out of `platforms` and its failures are not read [bar: strong]
- RULE-46: Where the feature's evidence holds no `audit` at all, a rule whose level is `passed` has a strong cell reading `weak` with the reason `no audit has run`, because only an audit measures how good the tests are [bar: strong]
- RULE-47: The signed cell carries `at` beside `signer`: the date of the commit that added the signature, and the file's own timestamp where git cannot answer [bar: strong]
- RULE-6: A proof carrying `@env` is proved only by a current section from that operating system: the passed cell reads `not run`, names the operating system under `missing_env` and gives the reason `<os>: no run yet` rather than taking a word of its own [bar: strong]
- RULE-7: Where the newest section that speaks for the rule is not current the passed cell reads `out of date`, not met and not current, with one reason per part that differs, `spec changed since <sha7>`, `code changed since <sha7>` or `tests changed since <sha7>` where the sha is the section's commit, and raises the `out_of_date` flag; a person's own section, committed or not, goes out of date like any other, and the next run clears it [bar: strong]
- RULE-8: A rule no test backs reads `no test` with no source, not current and not counting [bar: strong]
- RULE-9: The passed cell reads `failed` when a current section observed a failing proof of the rule, reading a `@env` proof only from that operating system's section; its reasons name where, as `failing: <os>, <source>`, and the `failing` flag is raised [bar: strong]
- RULE-10: The strong cell exists only at the gate `strong` and above and the signed cell only at `signed`; a cell above the gate is absent from `cells` rather than present and empty [bar: strong]
- RULE-11: A rule whose passed cell is not met and that nobody holds has a strong cell reading `weak` with the one reason `not passed`, and the feature's test strength beside it; a hold is read before the tests are, so a held rule reads `held` there instead [bar: strong]
- RULE-12: A test strength under `min_strength` makes the strong cell read `weak` with the one reason `strength <n>% under <m>%` [bar: strong]
- RULE-13: Where an audit ran and no engine measured a test strength the strong cell reads `strong` with the reason `no engine: nothing measured a strength` [bar: strong]
- RULE-14: The strong cell reads the audit entry for the rule's current rule, proof and test hashes, its `verdict` and its `findings`, and no other field: a field the format does not name decides nothing, a strong cell has no `findings` key at all, what makes it read `weak` is a finding of an entry that settled, which is among its reasons, and the cell names the evidence file under `evidence` [bar: strong]
- RULE-15: A rule whose level is `strong` or `signed` needs an audit entry for its current rule, proof and test hashes whose `verdict` settled; without one the strong cell reads `not audited` with the reason `no audit has run on this code`, an entry reading `undecided` reads `unsettled` with the reason `the AI audit could not settle`, and a rule whose level is `passed` reads neither word [bar: strong]
- RULE-16: A rule proved by a `@manual` proof reads `manual test` with the reason `manual proof`, and raises the `manual` flag [bar: strong]
- RULE-17: A current hold makes the strong cell read `held` with the reason `held by <email>: <case>` whatever the rule's level and whatever its tests are doing, makes the signed cell read `held`, raises the `held` flag, leaves the `manual`, `unsettled` and `not_audited` flags false, and keeps the rule from meeting the gate at `strong` and at `signed`; a hold bound to other hashes does nothing [bar: strong]
- RULE-18: A signature for the current hashes clears every reason a person was needed for, the hold included, because what it settles there is a question the machine could not [bar: strong]
- RULE-19: A strong cell that reads `strong` where an engine measured the strength carries no reasons at all [bar: passed]
- RULE-20: The signed cell is computed from the signature files whatever the cells below it read: `signed` with the reason `by <email>`, `unsigned`, `stale` with the reason `hashes changed after the signature`, or `held` [bar: strong]
- RULE-21: At the gate `signed` a rule needs a signature exactly when its level is `signed`; the signed cell of a rule whose level is lower is computed from the signature files and shown like any other, and does not keep the rule from meeting the gate [bar: strong]
- RULE-22: A signature the gate refuses leaves the signed cell at `unsigned` carrying the reason the check gave, `the signing commit is not signed`, which is the one reason the gate `signed` refuses a committed signature for [bar: strong]
- RULE-23: A rule's bucket is exactly one of `untested`, `failing`, `partial`, `passed`, `strong` and `signed`: `untested` while no counting run reads as passed, a rule no proof line names included, `failing` where every platform that ran failed, `partial` where they disagree, and otherwise the deepest level the rule met up to the gate [bar: strong]
- RULE-24: `meets_gate` is true when no cell up to the gate blocks the rule, and `blocked_by` names the lowest one that does, in the order `passed`, `strong`, `signed`, and is null when the rule meets the gate [bar: strong]
- RULE-25: A feature's rollup carries `rules`, `met`, one count per bucket the gate reaches, `stale`, `held`, `manual`, `unsettled`, `not_audited`, `signable`, the test strength, `proofs`, `proofs_without_test` and `proofs_without_test_ids` [bar: strong]
- RULE-48: `proofs` counts every proof line the rollup's rules write, once each, and `proofs_without_test` counts those of them that no test backs, leaving out a `@manual` proof, whose spec already says no test is written for it [bar: strong]
- RULE-26: The project summary counts each rule once, under the feature that owns it, so a global anchor's rule is counted once however many features have to prove it, and it adds `features` to the rollup's keys [bar: strong]
- RULE-27: The payload carries schema version 9 and the top-level keys `generated_at`, `generated_by`, `project`, `version`, `commit`, `dirty`, `gate`, `summary`, `features`, `review_list`, `sign_list`, `evidence`, `tag`, `remote_url` and `warnings`; `remote_url` is the `origin` remote, or null when the project has no remote [bar: strong]
- RULE-28: A feature entry carries its spec path, its category, the paths of the signature files beside its spec, `current`, true when its newest section is current, and `evidence`, which per source is null or the file's path, whether it is committed as it stands and each section's commit, time and whether it is current, and each of its rules with its `level`, the `level_marked` the spec tagged and the audit hash a signature binds, and each proof with whether it is `@manual`, its operating system and no findings key [bar: strong]
- RULE-29: The review list holds exactly the rules whose strong cell reads `manual test`, `unsettled` or `held`, each row naming the feature, the owner, the rule, the level, the cell, the kind and why; a rule reading `not audited` is never on it and the list is empty under the gate `passed` [bar: strong]
- RULE-30: A row's `kind` is the cell's own word, drawn from `manual test`, `unsettled` and `held` on the review list and from `unsigned`, `stale` and `held` on the sign list, and `why` carries that one word [bar: strong]
- RULE-31: Both lists read the rules whose level asks the most first, `signed`, then `strong`, then `passed`, then by feature and rule number, and name each rule once under the feature that owns it [bar: strong]
- RULE-32: The dashboard's data file is written as `const PURLIN_DATA = ` followed by the payload and a trailing semicolon, and reads back as the payload that was written [bar: strong]
- RULE-33: A rebuild whose payload differs from the file on disk only by the stamp on it touches that file instead of rewriting it, so a background refresh never churns the bytes [bar: strong]
- RULE-34: Every section in the payload's `evidence` map, keyed by feature, source and operating system, carries the result of the run it names, `fail` where any proof it observed failed and `pass` otherwise, beside its commit, its time, whether it is current and its path [bar: strong]
- RULE-35: The tests a rule's test hash binds for a proof are the ones the feature's current sections list, every operating system and both sources together, so every checkout and CI read the same hash; where no section is current every section's list answers, so a code change alone does not move the hash, and a runtime proof file this checkout wrote moves nothing [bar: strong]
- RULE-36: The status table is one row per spec under the columns Spec, Rules, Proofs and Tests, with Strong added at the gate `strong` and Signable and Signed added at `signed`; `Proofs` reads `<n>` and appends `· <k> without a test`, `Tests` reads `<passed> of <rules>` and appends `· <k> partial` and `· <k> failing`, `Strong` reads `<n> of <rules> · <strength>` and a feature with no audit shows `n/a`, and `Signable` and `Signed` each read `<n> of <rules>` [bar: strong]
- RULE-49: The status table and the dashboard render one spec's row from one module, so a cell reads the same characters wherever it is printed [bar: strong]
- RULE-37: The status report's summary opens `<met> of <rules> rules meet the gate <gate>`, then counts the buckets the gate reaches in the tiles' own words and order, then names a test strength or a signature rule only at the gate that asks for one [bar: strong]
- RULE-38: The report ends with exactly one `Next:` line, computed from the lowest cell blocking the project rather than from what the caller asked for [bar: strong]
- RULE-58: The `Next:` line for rules with no current run to read names `purlin:audit` at `strong` and at `signed`, because evidence either source wrote counts at both and the audit runs the tests too, and names `purlin:test --remote` instead where the project's `trust` is `remote`, which is the one setting that asks for a `ci` run; the `Review list:` line beside it reads the one sentence every surface says a person is owed in, so one rule reads `1 rule needs a person` [bar: strong]
- RULE-39: A project with no specs under `specs/` says what to run instead of printing an empty table [bar: passed]
- RULE-40: The report carries no emoji: the only characters above U+2000 it prints are the arrow, the two triangles and the horizontal rule the design system allows [bar: strong]
- RULE-41: A config carrying a key this release no longer reads prints the line `Run: purlin:init --update` above the next step [bar: strong]
- RULE-50: A rule's level is the lower of its `[level: ...]` tag and the gate, because the gate is the ceiling, and the gate where it names none; the rule entry carries `level`, the effective one, and `level_marked`, the tag as the spec wrote it or null [bar: strong]
- RULE-52: A rule meets the gate when its passed cell is met, its strong cell is met if its level is `strong` or `signed`, and its signed cell is met if its level is `signed`; a cell above the rule's level is still computed and shown, and does not block it [bar: strong]
- RULE-53: A rule is `signable` when its level is `signed`, its passed and strong cells are met and its signed cell does not read `signed`; the rollup and the summary carry `signable` as a count [bar: strong]
- RULE-54: The sign list holds exactly the signable rules, exists only at the gate `signed`, and each row names the feature, the owner, the rule, the level, the cell `signed`, the kind and why [bar: strong]
- RULE-55: At the gate `strong` and above the status report prints `<n> rules to review`, and at `signed` it prints `<n> rules to sign` under it, each counting its own list [bar: passed]
- RULE-60: Where a rule is marked above the gate the status report prints `<n> rules are marked above the gate and are read as <gate>.`, and `1 rule is marked above the gate and is read as <gate>.` for one; where none is, it prints neither [bar: strong]
- RULE-59: `tag` names the `signed/*` tag pointing at HEAD as `{name, commit}`, and is null where none does, so a surface that shows one commit's standing can say whether that commit is marked; where several point at HEAD the newest version wins, read as numbers where the name is numeric [bar: strong]
