The user opened a rule from the review list on the built dashboard and reported:

## 1. Where the Rule tab sits, and how it closes

`scripts/report/src/app.js` `tabs()` puts the open rule's tab between Board and Review list.
It goes to the right of Review list. The Rule screen also gets one link at its top,
`← Review list` (the glyph is one of the four allowed), which closes the rule (clears
`VIEW.rule` and `VIEW.feature`) and shows the review list; when the rule was opened from
the Board rather than the review list, the link reads `← Board` and returns there. Keep
the tab itself clickable while open.

## 2. The Review panel's sentences

Today: `On the review list because risk high needs a look. The free checks name no
negative case.` Rewrite `scripts/report/src/rule.js` so the panel says, in two short
sentences a QA person reads cold: why the rule is on the review list (from the payload's
`review_list[].reason`, in words: "Its risk is high, so a person looks before it can be
approved." or the reason the payload gives), then what the free checks found, each finding
in plain words with the proof id ("PROOF-3 states no negative case: every proof describes
the success path and none a rejection."). Read `scripts/mcp/purlin/checks.py` `describe()` for
the sentences the package already has and reuse them rather than inventing a second set.
When nothing is on the list and nothing was found, say so in one sentence.

## 3. Approving is not a button, and the page says why

Under `approved` an approval is a signed commit by someone on the approver list, bound to
the rule, proof and test hashes; a static page cannot make one, and that is by design
(A2: the dashboard is read-only). The Rule screen gets a "To approve" line under the
Review panel that shows the exact command for this rule in a `CommandChip`-style mono span:
`purlin:approve <feature> <RULE-N>` (from Claude Code), and one sentence: "An approval is a
signed commit by someone on the approver list; the page shows it once it is on the branch."
When the rule is already Approved, the line shows who approved it and when, from the
payload's approvals.
